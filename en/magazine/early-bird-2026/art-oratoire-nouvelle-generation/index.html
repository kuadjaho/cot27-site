Public speaking

The new generation of West African public speaking

From Cotonou's university clubs to the stages of Lagos, a generation of speakers is redefining public speaking in West Africa. COT27 will be their stage.

By Rédaction COT27Published on October 1, 20261 min read

Contents

  • Clubs bursting at the seams
  • Contests as a springboard

They are under thirty, juggle French, English and their mother tongues, and consider the stage a right rather than a privilege. District 130's new generation of speakers does not ask for the floor: it takes it.

Clubs bursting at the seams

In the universities of Abomey-Calavi, Ouagadougou or Douala, Toastmasters clubs are full. Sessions fill lecture halls, waiting lists grow, and internal contests draw audiences beyond the walls.

Contests as a springboard

COT27's speech contests — international speech, table topics, humorous speech, in French and in English — will crown the district's best speakers. For many, it is the first step toward international stages.

When I step on stage, I carry the voice of everyone who was never listened to.

In Cotonou, in May 2027, this generation will have one week, one stage and twelve countries to make itself heard. The date is set.
